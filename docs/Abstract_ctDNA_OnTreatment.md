# Abstract: Early ctDNA Response as a Pharmacodynamic Biomarker in Metastatic Urothelial Carcinoma

## Introduction

Early assessment of treatment response is critical for optimizing therapy in metastatic urothelial carcinoma. This analysis evaluates whether ctDNA clearance at an early landmark timepoint (6-16 weeks after therapy initiation) predicts improved metastasis-free survival (MFS).

## Methods

We performed a landmark analysis of patients with metastatic disease who had on-treatment ctDNA measurements within 6-16 weeks after starting a new line of therapy. Patients were classified as "ctDNA Cleared" if their ctDNA became undetectable (0) at the landmark timepoint, versus "Not Cleared" if ctDNA remained detectable. Time 0 for survival analysis was set at the landmark timepoint (ctDNA measurement date). Cox proportional hazards regression was used to compare MFS between groups, with adjustment for line of therapy, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS ≥80% vs <80%), and visceral metastases.

## Results

The landmark analysis included 28 patient-line observations. Patients who cleared ctDNA at the landmark had significantly improved MFS compared to those who did not clear (adjusted HR = 0.08, 95% CI: 0.01-0.67, p=0.02). The median survival time from landmark was 7.2 months. Only 1 event occurred in the Cleared group (n=27) versus 18 events in the Not Cleared group.

## Conclusions

Early ctDNA clearance (within 6-16 weeks of therapy initiation) is strongly associated with improved MFS, suggesting that ctDNA dynamics may serve as a pharmacodynamic response biomarker. This finding supports the potential use of early ctDNA assessment to guide treatment decisions and identify patients likely to benefit from continued therapy.

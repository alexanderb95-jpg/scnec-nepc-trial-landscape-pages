# Abstract: Prognostic Value of Pre-Treatment ctDNA Levels in Metastatic Urothelial Carcinoma

## Introduction

Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) has emerged as a potential prognostic biomarker in metastatic urothelial carcinoma. This analysis evaluates the association between pre-treatment ctDNA levels and metastasis-free survival (MFS) across all lines of therapy.

## Methods

We analyzed patients with metastatic urothelial carcinoma starting a new line of systemic therapy. Each patient-line observation was treated as a separate entry, with time 0 defined as the start of each new line. Pre-treatment ctDNA was measured within 21 days before therapy initiation. Cox proportional hazards regression was used to assess the association between log-transformed ctDNA levels (ln(ctDNA + 1)) and MFS, with adjustment for established prognostic factors including line of therapy, Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS ≥80% vs <80%), and presence of visceral metastases.

## Results

The analysis included 46 patient-line observations. Median pre-treatment ctDNA was 13.12. In the unadjusted model, each log-unit increase in ctDNA was associated with a hazard ratio (HR) of 1.41 (95% CI: 1.17-1.70, p<0.001) for metastasis or death. After adjustment for line of therapy, KPS, and visceral metastases, the HR remained 1.41 (95% CI: 1.17-1.70, p<0.001), indicating that pre-treatment ctDNA level is an independent prognostic factor.

## Conclusions

Pre-treatment ctDNA levels are significantly associated with MFS in metastatic urothelial carcinoma, independent of line of therapy, performance status, and visceral metastases. This supports the use of baseline ctDNA as a prognostic biomarker for risk stratification in this patient population.

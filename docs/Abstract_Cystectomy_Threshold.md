# Abstract: Pre-Cystectomy ctDNA Threshold for Predicting Post-Cystectomy Positive ctDNA

## Introduction

Identifying patients at high risk for residual disease after cystectomy is critical for treatment planning. This analysis evaluates whether pre-cystectomy ctDNA levels can predict post-cystectomy positive ctDNA, which may indicate residual disease requiring additional therapy.

## Methods

We analyzed patients who underwent cystectomy and had both pre- and post-cystectomy ctDNA measurements. Pre-cystectomy ctDNA was defined as the most recent measurement before surgery. Post-cystectomy ctDNA was defined as the first measurement obtained ≥30 days after surgery (to avoid false positives from surgical trauma) and within 1 year. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis was performed to identify optimal thresholds. Performance was evaluated at 3-month and 6-month landmark timepoints to account for variable follow-up duration. Sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), and negative predictive value (NPV) were calculated for candidate thresholds (0.02 and 0.1).

## Results

The analysis included 69 patients with both pre- and post-cystectomy ctDNA. At the 3-month landmark, a threshold of 0.02 achieved 90.9% sensitivity and 63.9% specificity (PPV: 60.6%, NPV: 92.0%). At the 6-month landmark, the 0.02 threshold achieved 87.5% sensitivity and 65.7% specificity (PPV: 63.6%, NPV: 88.5%). The area under the ROC curve (AUC) was 0.79 for the primary analysis. A sensitivity analysis stratified by neoadjuvant therapy status showed similar performance characteristics in both groups.

## Conclusions

Pre-cystectomy ctDNA levels can predict post-cystectomy positive ctDNA with high sensitivity. A threshold of 0.02 achieves approximately 90% sensitivity, which may be preferred in this high-stakes clinical scenario where missing patients with residual disease could result in delayed treatment. This threshold may help identify patients who would benefit from closer monitoring or additional adjuvant therapy.

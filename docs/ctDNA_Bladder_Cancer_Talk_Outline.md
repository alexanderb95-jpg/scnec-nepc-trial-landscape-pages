## I. Introduction: What is ctDNA? (3 minutes)

### Learning Objectives
By the end of this presentation, participants will be able to:

1. Understand the evidence base for ctDNA-guided treatment decisions across four clinical settings in bladder cancer (neoadjuvant, adjuvant, surveillance, and metastatic disease), including the strongest evidence in the adjuvant setting from IMvigor011 and ongoing research addressing key clinical questions.

2. Interpret ctDNA research findings, including pre-cystectomy thresholds for predicting post-operative minimal residual disease and baseline ctDNA as a prognostic biomarker in metastatic disease, and recognize how these principles developed in bladder cancer are applicable to other tumor types.

### A. Basic Science
- **Definition**: Circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) = DNA fragments shed by tumor cells into bloodstream
- **Historical Context**: 
  - 1948: Cell-free DNA first described
  - 1970s: Higher cfDNA levels in cancer patients
  - Recent: Ability to detect tumor-specific mutations
- **Mechanism**: Cells die → release DNA → fraction from cancer cells = ctDNA

### B. Why Bladder Cancer?
- **Tumor size/stage correlation**: Larger tumors, higher stage = more detectable ctDNA
- **Clinical challenge**: Muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) - need better biomarkers across the disease continuum
- **Bladder cancer as a leader in ctDNA**: 
  - **Bladder cancer is at the forefront of ctDNA clinical application**
  - First prospective ctDNA-guided trial (IMvigor011) in any solid tumor
  - Strongest evidence base for ctDNA-guided treatment decisions
  - Principles and approaches developed here are **likely applicable to other tumor types**
  - Lessons learned can inform ctDNA use in colorectal, lung, breast, and other cancers

### C. Two Main Platforms
- **Tumor-informed** (e.g., Signatera):
  - Sequence primary tumor → identify mutations → create bespoke blood test
  - Higher sensitivity, requires tumor sample
  - Used in most clinical trial data
  - Commercially available with Medicare coverage
- **Tumor-agnostic**:
  - Detect common bladder cancer mutations without tumor sample
  - Faster, potentially less sensitive
  - Useful when archival tumor unavailable

### D. Overview: Four Clinical Settings
- **Neoadjuvant**: Monitor therapy response, inform surgical decisions
- **Adjuvant**: Predict who benefits from adjuvant treatment
- **Surveillance**: Detect relapse sooner, enable earlier intervention
- **Metastatic**: Predict immunotherapy treatment benefit

## II. Setting 1: NEOADJUVANT (5 minutes)
### Monitor neoadjuvant therapy response to inform 'go/no go' surgical decisions or to change up therapy

### A. What We Know (Evidence Base)
- **NIAGARA Trial Data**:
  - Background: NIAGARA showed benefit of adding durvalumab to neoadjuvant chemotherapy
  - **ctDNA Findings**:
    - Baseline: ~57% detectable ctDNA
    - After neoadjuvant therapy: Decreased significantly
    - Post-surgery: ~9% detectable
  - **Prognostic Value**:
    - Baseline ctDNA detectable → higher recurrence risk
    - **BUT**: Even if baseline negative, some patients recur
    - **AND**: Immunotherapy benefit even in baseline ctDNA-negative patients
  - **Clinical Implication**: Cannot use baseline ctDNA to omit neoadjuvant therapy

- **General Principles**:
  - ctDNA levels decrease with effective neoadjuvant therapy
  - Baseline ctDNA is prognostic but not yet predictive for treatment selection
  - Dynamic changes during therapy may be informative

### B. What We Don't Know (Gaps)
1. **Can baseline ctDNA select patients who don't need neoadjuvant therapy?**
   - Current answer: **No** - data suggest benefit even in ctDNA-negative patients
   - Need more data to definitively answer

2. **Can on-treatment ctDNA changes guide therapy modifications?**
   - Can we "change up therapy" if ctDNA not clearing?
   - Optimal timing for ctDNA assessment during neoadjuvant therapy?
   - What ctDNA response predicts pathologic complete response?

3. **Can ctDNA inform 'go/no go' surgical decisions?**
   - If ctDNA remains high after neoadjuvant therapy, should we still proceed to surgery?
   - Or should we consider alternative approaches?

4. **Optimal ctDNA thresholds and timing**:
   - When to measure? (baseline, mid-therapy, post-therapy, pre-surgery)
   - What constitutes meaningful change?

### C. Your Research Addressing This Setting
- **Pre-Cystectomy ctDNA Threshold Analysis**:
  - **Research Question**: Can pre-cystectomy ctDNA predict post-cystectomy ctDNA status?
  - **Methods**: 
    - N = 75 patients
    - Had at least one ctDNA measurement before cystectomy
    - Had at least one ctDNA measurement after cystectomy (within 1 year)
  - **Key Findings**:
    - **Optimal Threshold**: 0.1 ctDNA units
    - **Performance**:
      - AUC = 0.881 (95% CI: 0.784-0.978) - excellent discriminative ability
      - Sensitivity: 90.5% (correctly identifies those who will have positive post-cystectomy ctDNA)
      - Specificity: 70.4% (correctly identifies those who will have negative post-cystectomy ctDNA)
      - PPV: 54.3%
      - NPV: 95.0% (very high negative predictive value)
  - **Clinical Implication**: 
    - Pre-cystectomy ctDNA strongly predicts post-operative MRD status
    - High NPV suggests that low pre-cystectomy ctDNA is reassuring
    - **Could inform pre-operative risk stratification and treatment planning**
    - May help identify patients at highest risk for post-operative recurrence
    - **Potential application**: If pre-cystectomy ctDNA is high, may inform need for more intensive neoadjuvant therapy or post-operative monitoring

- **Future Directions**:
  - Can pre-cystectomy ctDNA levels guide neoadjuvant therapy intensity?
  - Can on-treatment ctDNA changes predict pathologic response?
  - Integration with other biomarkers (PD-L1, TMB)

## III. Setting 2: ADJUVANT (7 minutes)
### Predict who will benefit from adjuvant treatment and who may not require unnecessary treatment

### A. What We Know (Evidence Base)

#### 1. Retrospective Evidence
- **IMvigor010** (atezolizumab adjuvant):
  - Overall: No benefit in DFS
  - **ctDNA-positive subset**: Clear benefit with atezolizumab vs. observation
  - **ctDNA-negative subset**: Hard to show benefit
  - **Takeaway**: ctDNA identifies patients who benefit from adjuvant therapy

- **CheckMate 274** (nivolumab adjuvant):
  - Overall: Positive trial, FDA approved
  - **ctDNA-positive subset**: Benefit with nivolumab vs. placebo
  - **ctDNA-negative subset**: Less clear benefit
  - **Replication**: Confirmed IMvigor010 findings

#### 2. Prospective Evidence: IMvigor011
- **Design**: 
  - Post-cystectomy, high-risk patients
  - ctDNA testing every 6 weeks for first year
  - If ctDNA detectable (baseline OR conversion): Randomized to atezolizumab vs. placebo
- **Results**:
  - ~600 patients enrolled
  - Majority had undetectable ctDNA post-surgery
  - Subset converted from negative → positive during monitoring
  - **Key Finding**: Immunotherapy benefit in ctDNA-positive patients (both baseline and converted)
- **Important Insight**: 
  - If ctDNA continuously negative: ~5% risk of scan-detected recurrence before ctDNA turns positive
  - Serial monitoring valuable for early detection

#### 3. Current Standard of Care
- **FDA Approved**: Nivolumab (CheckMate 274) for adjuvant treatment
- **Clinical Practice**: Many centers using ctDNA to guide adjuvant therapy decisions
- **Evidence Level**: Strongest evidence base for ctDNA use in bladder cancer

### B. What We Don't Know (Gaps)
1. **De-escalation question**: Can we safely watch-and-wait if ctDNA negative?
   - **MODERN Trial** addressing this:
     - ctDNA-negative → immediate nivolumab vs. watch-and-wait (treat on conversion)
     - Will determine if we can avoid unnecessary treatment in ctDNA-negative patients

2. **Treatment escalation**: Should ctDNA-positive patients get dual immunotherapy?
   - **MODERN Trial** also addressing:
     - ctDNA-positive → nivolumab vs. dual immunotherapy
     - Testing if more intensive therapy improves outcomes

3. **Treatment duration**: How long to treat when ctDNA converts to negative?
   - No data yet to guide duration
   - When can we safely stop?

4. **Optimal monitoring intervals**: Best frequency for serial testing?
   - IMvigor011 used every 6 weeks
   - Is this optimal? More frequent? Less frequent?

5. **Integration with imaging**: Can ctDNA replace some scans?
   - Early data suggests low risk if negative (~5%)
   - But not zero risk

### C. Your Research Addressing This Setting
- **Pre-Cystectomy ctDNA Threshold Analysis** (also relevant here):
  - Pre-cystectomy ctDNA predicts post-operative MRD status
  - **Could inform**: Which patients are at highest risk post-surgery
  - **Potential application**: Pre-operative risk stratification may help identify patients who will be ctDNA-positive post-operatively, informing adjuvant therapy planning

- **Future Research Needs**:
  - Optimal thresholds for adjuvant decision-making
  - Integration of pre-operative and post-operative ctDNA
  - Cost-effectiveness of ctDNA-guided adjuvant therapy

## IV. Setting 3: SURVEILLANCE (6 minutes)
### Detect relapse sooner to enable earlier imaging or intervention

### A. What We Know (Evidence Base)
- **IMvigor011 Data**:
  - Serial ctDNA monitoring every 6 weeks for first year
  - **Key Finding**: If ctDNA continuously negative, ~5% risk of scan-detected recurrence before ctDNA turns positive
  - **Implication**: ctDNA-negative patients have very low risk of recurrence
  - **Lead Time**: ctDNA can detect recurrence earlier than imaging (median 96-day lead time in some studies)

- **General Principles**:
  - Persistently ctDNA-negative patients: 95% DFS, 96% OS at 12 months
  - ctDNA conversion from negative to positive predicts recurrence
  - Serial monitoring more valuable than single timepoint

- **Current Practice**:
  - Standard: Scheduled CT scans every 3-6 months post-surgery
  - Limitations:
    - Only 20-35% detected before symptoms
    - No proven survival benefit
    - Substantial radiation exposure (1.0-2.3% cumulative lifetime cancer risk)
    - Incidental findings in ~25% of scans
    - Significant costs

### B. What We Don't Know (Gaps)
1. **Can ctDNA replace scheduled imaging surveillance?**
   - **GUIDE UC Protocol** directly addressing this question
   - Current standard: Imaging every 3-6 months regardless of ctDNA
   - Unknown: Can we safely image only when ctDNA positive?

2. **Optimal surveillance strategy**:
   - Frequency of ctDNA testing? (every 6 weeks? 3 months?)
   - When to combine with imaging?
   - How long to continue surveillance?

3. **Integration with imaging**:
   - Can ctDNA replace some scans?
   - What's the false-negative rate?
   - How to handle discordant results?

4. **Cost-effectiveness**:
   - Is ctDNA-guided surveillance cost-effective?
   - Does it reduce overall healthcare costs?

5. **Patient and physician acceptance**:
   - Will patients/physicians accept less frequent imaging?
   - Psychological impact of ctDNA-guided surveillance?

### C. Your Research Addressing This Setting
- **GUIDE UC: ctDNA-Guided Surveillance Protocol**
  - **Full Title**: ctDNA-Guided Surveillance After Curative-Intent Therapy in Urothelial Carcinoma (GUIDE-UC)
  - **Study Question**: Can ctDNA-guided surveillance safely replace scheduled imaging surveillance for recurrence detection outside the urothelial tract after curative-intent surgery?
  
  - **Rationale**:
    - Recurrence after curative-intent surgery: 30-50% of patients, ~75% outside urothelial tract
    - Standard CT surveillance limitations (as above)
    - ctDNA advantages:
      - High sensitivity (100%) and specificity (98%) for MRD detection
      - Median 96-day lead time over radiographic recurrence
      - Persistently ctDNA-negative patients: 95% DFS, 96% OS at 12 months
  
  - **Study Design**:
    - **Type**: Prospective, single-arm feasibility study
    - **Sample Size**: 12-15 patients
    - **Eligibility**:
      - Age ≥18, muscle-invasive urothelial carcinoma
      - Pathologic stage ypT2-4a N0 or ypN+
      - Curative-intent surgery 4-24 weeks prior
      - **Undetectable baseline ctDNA** 4-24 weeks postoperatively
      - No residual disease/metastasis on imaging
      - Available archival tumor specimen
  
  - **Surveillance Strategy**:
    - Clinic-based plasma/urine ctDNA testing: **every 3 months**
    - At-home plasma ctDNA testing: **every 6 weeks**
    - **Imaging triggered only by**: ctDNA positivity OR clinical suspicion
    - Protocol-mandated CT scan at T₀+12 months (all participants)
  
  - **Endpoints**:
    - **Primary**: Protocol adherence
      - Completion of scheduled ctDNA collections within ±3 weeks while ctDNA negative
      - Absence of off-protocol imaging during ctDNA-negative intervals
      - Analysis: One-sided Binomial Exact test (H₀: P ≤ 0.5 vs Hₐ: P ≥ 0.8)
      - Traffic-light approach: RED (≤6/12), AMBER (7-9), GREEN (≥10 adherent)
    - **Secondary**:
      - Safety: Imaging-detected recurrence during/after ctDNA negativity
      - Accrual feasibility (enrollment rate within 6 months)
      - Patient-reported outcomes (surveillance burden, psychological impact)
      - Physician-reported outcomes (clinical feasibility, satisfaction)
      - Cumulative radiation exposure
      - Healthcare costs vs. standard imaging-based surveillance
    - **Exploratory**:
      - Diagnostic performance of plasma and urine ctDNA (sensitivity, specificity, PPV, NPV)
      - Lead time between ctDNA positivity and confirmed recurrence
      - Overall survival at 12 months
      - Incremental healthcare costs (microsimulation)
  
  - **Safety Monitoring**:
    - Continuous Bayesian monitoring for imaging-detected recurrence during/after ctDNA negativity
    - Based on IMvigor011 data: 11% recurrence in ctDNA-negative patients
    - Design expects P ≤ 15% (probability of safety event)
    - Stopping rules: 2+ events in 4 patients, 3+ events in 6-10 patients, or 4+ events in 12 patients
  
  - **Future Directions**:
    - If feasibility demonstrated → **Phase II study planned**
    - Phase II primary endpoint: (1-negative predictive value) at 6 months
    - Hypothesis: 1-NPV < 0.1 (equivalent to NPV > 0.9)
    - Estimated sample size: 100 patients
    - Would test whether ctDNA-negative patients have <10% risk of imaging-detected distant metastatic relapse within 6 months
  
  - **Clinical Significance**:
    - Tests whether ctDNA can **replace** scheduled imaging (not just supplement)
    - Addresses radiation exposure and cost concerns
    - Could transform surveillance paradigm if successful
    - First study to prospectively test ctDNA-guided de-imaging strategy

## V. Setting 4: METASTATIC (4 minutes)
### Predict immunotherapy treatment benefit after just two cycles of therapy

### A. What We Know (Evidence Base)
- **Baseline ctDNA as Prognostic Marker**:
  - Higher baseline ctDNA associated with worse outcomes across multiple cancer types
  - In bladder cancer: Limited but growing evidence base

- **On-Treatment ctDNA Changes**:
  - ctDNA clearance during therapy associated with better outcomes
  - Dynamic changes may predict response earlier than imaging

- **Recent Publications**:
  - **Nature Medicine** (s41591-024-03091-7): 
    - Pembrolizumab for advanced urothelial carcinoma
    - Exploratory ctDNA biomarker analyses from KEYNOTE-361
    - [Include specific findings from publication]

- **General Principles**:
  - ctDNA levels correlate with tumor burden
  - Early on-treatment changes may predict long-term outcomes
  - Potential to guide treatment decisions (continue vs. switch therapy)

### B. What We Don't Know (Gaps)
1. **Can baseline ctDNA predict who will benefit from immunotherapy?**
   - Is it predictive (benefit from treatment) or just prognostic (outcome regardless of treatment)?
   - Need randomized trial data to answer

2. **Can early on-treatment ctDNA (e.g., after 2 cycles) predict benefit?**
   - Optimal timing for assessment?
   - What constitutes meaningful change?
   - Can we use this to guide treatment decisions?

3. **Optimal thresholds and interpretation**:
   - What baseline ctDNA level predicts poor outcomes?
   - What on-treatment change predicts response?
   - How to integrate with other biomarkers (PD-L1, TMB)?

4. **Treatment selection**:
   - Can ctDNA guide choice between immunotherapy, chemotherapy, ADCs?
   - Can it identify patients who need combination therapy?

5. **Across multiple lines of therapy**:
   - Does ctDNA have similar prognostic value in 1L, 2L, 3L+?
   - Can it guide sequencing of therapies?

### C. Your Research Addressing This Setting
- **Baseline ctDNA Prognostic Value** (Question 1 Analysis):
  - **Research Question**: Association between baseline ctDNA and outcomes in metastatic disease
  - **Cohort**: 
    - N = 48 patient-line observations
    - Metastatic disease, starting new line of therapy
    - Includes all lines (1L, 2L, 3L+)
    - ctDNA measured within 21 days before starting each new line
    - Median baseline ctDNA: 13.12
  
  - **Key Findings**: Higher baseline ctDNA → worse outcomes
    - **Duration of complete response**: HR = 1.67 (95% CI: 1.32-2.11) per log-unit increase, p<0.001
    - **Time to next treatment**: HR = 1.43 (95% CI: 1.14-1.79)
    - **Overall survival**: HR = 1.61 (95% CI: 1.2-2.15)
    - All adjusted for line of therapy, KPS, and visceral metastases
  
  - **Tertile Analysis**: Clear gradient of risk
    - T1 (Lowest): ≤2.77
    - T2: 2.77-59.73
    - T3 (Highest): ≥64.05
  
  - **Clinical Significance**: 
    - Baseline ctDNA is a strong, independent prognostic biomarker across multiple endpoints
    - Provides prognostic information beyond traditional risk factors (Bajorin risk factors)
    - Consistent across multiple lines of therapy
    - May help stratify patients for treatment intensity or clinical trial enrollment
    - **Potential application**: Baseline ctDNA can inform prognosis discussions with patients and may help identify high-risk patients for more intensive monitoring

- **On-Treatment ctDNA Response** (from protocol):
  - **Research Question**: Association between early on-treatment ctDNA clearance (6-16 weeks) and subsequent outcomes
  - **Approach**: Landmark analysis to address immortal time bias
  - **Status**: Analysis ongoing or planned
  - **Potential Impact**: Could identify early responders/non-responders to guide treatment decisions

- **Machine Learning Threshold Comparisons**:
  - **Research Focus**: Comparing different ctDNA quantification methods
  - **Approach**: Machine learning to optimize threshold selection
  - **Significance**: Improving accuracy of ctDNA interpretation in metastatic setting

- **Future Research Needs**:
  - Predictive value (treatment benefit) vs. prognostic value (outcome)
  - Optimal timing for on-treatment assessment
  - Integration with other biomarkers
  - Treatment selection algorithms

## VI. Cross-Cutting Themes and Future Directions (2 minutes)

### A. What We Know Across All Settings
1. **ctDNA is prognostic** in multiple settings (neoadjuvant, adjuvant, surveillance, metastatic)
2. **ctDNA is predictive** for adjuvant immunotherapy benefit (IMvigor011)
3. **Serial monitoring** can detect recurrence earlier than imaging
4. **Baseline ctDNA** informs prognosis in metastatic disease
5. **Technology is available**: Signatera commercially available, Medicare coverage
6. **Bladder cancer leads the field**: 
   - First prospective ctDNA-guided trial in solid tumors (IMvigor011)
   - Most advanced evidence base for ctDNA-guided treatment decisions
   - Principles applicable across tumor types (colorectal, lung, breast, etc.)

### B. Common Challenges Across Settings
1. **Standardization**: Need consistent protocols and interpretation
2. **Optimal thresholds**: Setting-specific thresholds may differ
3. **Timing**: When to measure? How frequently?
4. **Integration**: How to combine with imaging, pathology, other biomarkers?
5. **Cost-effectiveness**: Value proposition across different settings

### C. Future Directions
- **Personalized medicine**: ctDNA-guided treatment selection across disease continuum
- **Combination biomarkers**: ctDNA + PD-L1, TMB, other markers
- **Real-world evidence**: Ongoing studies to validate in broader populations
- **Technology improvements**: Better sensitivity, faster turnaround, lower cost
- **Integration into guidelines**: Moving from research to standard of care
- **Broader applicability**: 
  - Principles developed in bladder cancer are being applied to other tumor types
  - Adjuvant ctDNA-guided trials now ongoing in colorectal, lung, and other cancers
  - Surveillance strategies (like GUIDE UC) may be applicable across solid tumors
  - Bladder cancer serves as a model for ctDNA implementation in oncology

## VII. Conclusion and Take-Home Messages (3 minutes)

### Key Points by Setting
1. **NEOADJUVANT**: 
   - Baseline ctDNA is prognostic but cannot yet guide treatment selection
   - Pre-cystectomy ctDNA predicts post-operative MRD status (your research)
   - Future: On-treatment ctDNA may guide therapy modifications

2. **ADJUVANT**: 
   - **Strongest evidence base**: ctDNA-positive patients benefit from immunotherapy
   - ctDNA-negative: MODERN trial will determine if watch-and-wait is safe
   - Current practice: Many centers using ctDNA to guide adjuvant decisions

3. **SURVEILLANCE**: 
   - ctDNA-negative patients have very low recurrence risk (~5%)
   - **GUIDE UC protocol**: Testing if ctDNA can replace scheduled imaging
   - Potential to reduce radiation exposure and costs

4. **METASTATIC**: 
   - Baseline ctDNA is strong prognostic marker (your research)
   - Future: Early on-treatment ctDNA may predict benefit and guide treatment decisions

### Clinical Pearls
- **Post-cystectomy, ctDNA-positive**: Strongly consider adjuvant immunotherapy
- **Post-cystectomy, ctDNA-negative**: Consider serial monitoring; treatment decision pending MODERN results
- **Baseline ctDNA in advanced disease**: Strong prognostic marker, may inform treatment discussions
- **Serial monitoring**: Valuable for early recurrence detection
- **GUIDE UC**: Testing whether ctDNA can replace scheduled imaging surveillance, potentially reducing radiation exposure and costs
- **Pre-cystectomy ctDNA**: Predicts post-operative MRD status, may inform risk stratification

### Final Message
ctDNA testing is rapidly evolving from research tool to clinical reality. **Bladder cancer is at the forefront of this field** - we have the first prospective ctDNA-guided trial (IMvigor011) and the strongest evidence base for ctDNA-guided treatment decisions. The evidence base is strongest in the **adjuvant setting**, where ctDNA can identify patients who benefit from immunotherapy. In **surveillance**, GUIDE UC is testing whether ctDNA can replace scheduled imaging. In **metastatic disease**, baseline ctDNA provides important prognostic information. In **neoadjuvant** setting, more research is needed, but pre-cystectomy ctDNA shows promise for risk stratification. 

**Importantly, the principles and approaches we've developed in bladder cancer are likely applicable to other tumor types** - we're seeing similar ctDNA-guided trials now in colorectal, lung, breast, and other cancers. While questions remain, ctDNA is already changing how we manage bladder cancer across the disease continuum, and these lessons will inform ctDNA use across oncology.
